Assignment 4 · Photo Composition

Street Level

Six photographs taken on foot, at eye level, on streets, stairways and pavements. Some were shot after dark, some in flat midday light, but all of them are about the same thing: what a city looks like from the ground, at the height of a person walking through it. Each frame uses one of the six compositional techniques from class, and each edit was made to push the eye toward the point the composition was already pointing at.

Reflection

The strongest image is Two Cars. The balance was almost accidental — a white van on one side, a blue car with a giant can on the other — but cropping the sky turned the frame into four shapes that answer each other: two vehicles above, two long shadows below. The edit did the rest. Contrast pulled the vehicles off the grey road, and cooling the asphalt separated them from the warm brick. Before the crop it read as a snapshot with a strip of empty sky across the top.

If I did the assignment again I would shoot each subject twice — once where I first saw it, once after moving two steps. Most of the improvement in this set came from position, not from editing. I would also wait for better light at the fruit stall. Flat midday sun forced me to push saturation hard, and the reds are close to clipping; late afternoon light would have done that work for me.
